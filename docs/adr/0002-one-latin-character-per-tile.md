# One Latin character per Tile, in every language

Indonesian, Sundanese and Javanese all use digraphs — `ng`, `ny`, and in Javanese `dh` and `th` — where two written characters spell one sound. We decided a Tile always holds exactly one Latin character, so KUCING is a six-Tile word, not five.

The linguistically honest alternative (NG as a single Tile) was rejected because it is genuinely ambiguous to type and to score. In SANGGUP, `n-g-g` could split as N+G+G or NG+G; in MENYANYI, NY may or may not be a unit. Resolving that needs a hand-curated tile-split stored per word, a bigger keyboard, and an answer to "does a correct NG also mark the N key as used?" None of that buys the player anything.

The cost we accept: a Javanese or Sundanese speaker may find splitting NG across two Tiles slightly unnatural. In exchange we get one game engine, one A-Z keyboard, and puzzle difficulty that is comparable across all four languages — which is what makes cross-language badges mean anything.
