Four ways to draw a year

The play history should show how much you played each day and which of the four languages. One small square can't carry both. Here are four answers on the same 371 days of made-up play. Hover any day.

Games 0 → 12
A

Intensity only

One cell = how many games, in one hue. Language lives in the tooltip. GitHub's model, unchanged.

Buys
Densest and most legible. Instantly familiar. Reads fine at a glance on a phone.
Costs
Language is invisible until you hover — so it's absent entirely on touch.
B

Hue is the language

One cell = the language you played most that day, shaded by how many games. Ties go to the language you played first.

Buys
Two facts per cell at full density. A month of Javanese is visible from across the room.
Costs
"Most" is a lie on mixed days — 2 English and 2 Javanese shows as one colour. Shade and hue compete.
C

Four quadrants

One cell = four sub-squares, one per language, each shaded by its own count. Nothing is hidden and nothing is averaged.

Buys
Complete. Every language on every day, no tie-breaking, no lost information.
Costs
Needs 16px cells to read, so the year no longer fits — it scrolls. Busy days turn to mud.
D

One row per language

Four thin calendars stacked, one per language, each in its own hue. Position carries the language; shade carries the count.

Buys
Unambiguous, and the only one where language survives without colour vision. Reads like a habit chart — gaps in one language are obvious.
Costs
Four times the height, and a language you've never touched is a permanently empty row.
Where I'd land

D for the profile page, A for anywhere small. D is the only one that keeps every fact without asking the reader to decode a colour, and empty rows are arguably a feature — they're an invitation to try Sundanese. A is what belongs in a card or a share image, where there's no room for four strips.

B is the tempting one and I'd avoid it: the tie-breaking rule is a fiction, and it puts the whole encoding on hue, which is exactly what fails for colourblind readers and in print. C is honest but only survives at a size that breaks the year.
